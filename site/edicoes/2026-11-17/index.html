Adaptive Fisher-Whitened Cross-Covariance for Low-Resource Speech Recognition

The method replaces generic LoRA adapters with a Fisher-whitened cross-covariance (FCCA) subspace derived from downstream task data, optionally with asymmetric cross-layer coupling (AC-FCCA) and adaptive per-matrix rank reallocation (AR-FCCA) under a fixed budget. Cost is a one-time covariance/Fisher estimation pass over task data plus whitened eigendecomposition per adapted layer, with no increase in trainable parameters or inference latency relative to LoRA. It can fail when downstream data is too scarce for stable covariance and Fisher estimation, when the task-informed subspace does not transfer across languages, and because results are demonstrated only on Whisper and Qwen3-ASR for ASR, so applicability to other modalities is unverified.

REMORY: Learning Residual Memory for Context Compaction

REMORY replaces the plain-text summary used in context compaction with a summary plus a bounded sequence of learned soft memory tokens, appended as a residual along the sequence dimension to recover information the summary dropped. The cost is training a separate memory network per backbone LLM against a frozen model's continuation objective, plus the inference-time overhead of generating the tokens, which demands gradient-level access to the target model during training and is impractical for API-only or 27B-class models on a single 24 GB GPU. It can fail when deployed on a backbone or domain different from the training setup, since the soft tokens are model-specific and their benefit is validated mainly on summarization-attribution and two agent benchmarks rather than general workloads.

Memory-Efficient Speculative Decoding with Quantized Draft KV Cache

This method replaces the BF16 key-value cache typically used by the draft model in speculative decoding with a quantized 4-bit cache (K4V4) using per-channel keys and per-token values. The cost is a negligible drop in mean acceptance rate (less than 1.4% relative), while significantly reducing VRAM overhead. Failure can occur if the quantizer layout is not co-designed with the bit-split; naive per-token quantization of keys causes severe acceptance degradation compared to the proposed grouped per-channel approach.

Contrastive Activation-Guided Zeroth-Order Preference Optimisation for Memory-Efficient LLM Alignment

The method replaces backpropagation-based DPO with zeroth-order optimisation in which perturbations are confined to a low-rank subspace built from the activation discrepancy between chosen and rejected responses, with momentum kept in low-rank coordinates. It eliminates activation-memory cost and cuts peak GPU memory by roughly 59% at sequence length 2048, at the price of the slower convergence and higher gradient-estimation variance typical of zeroth-order methods. Validation covers only a 1.5B model on one preference dataset, so results may not transfer to larger models, longer training runs, or other alignment objectives, and ZO training can require many more forward passes to match first-order quality.

Calibrate Once, Grow the Vocabulary: Candidate-Independent Scores for Conformal Risk Control in Open-Vocabulary Segmentation

The method replaces per-vocabulary conformal recalibration with scoring each candidate label against a fixed reference bank, making scores invariant to added class names so one calibration retains its risk guarantee as the vocabulary grows. It costs no training and little overhead, but prediction sets can grow by up to roughly one extra label per pixel, and the bounded miss rate for added names holds only if annotated names are a random sample of names in the calibration images, with excess bounded by the gap in mean miss rate otherwise. Latent Lattice Language Models

The Lattice replaces the standard Transformer residual stream (and the internal states of SSM/linear-attention hybrids) by adding persistent per-layer horizontal latent streams that directly condition attention, KV-cache writes, MLP computation, and decoding. It requires training from scratch with increased architectural complexity and a larger state footprint than parameter-matched baselines, though exact inference latency and memory costs are not quantified in the abstract. Adoption can fail because benefits are demonstrated only at the 406M scale on 20B tokens, and the recurrent latents may introduce unforeseen training instabilities or deployment overheads that negate perplexity gains at production scale.

Adaptive Fisher-Whitened Cross-Covariance for Low-Resource Speech Recognition

efficient training · test · 1 of 2 implementations is independent

The method replaces generic LoRA adapters with a Fisher-whitened cross-covariance (FCCA) subspace derived from downstream task data, optionally with asymmetric cross-layer coupling (AC-FCCA) and adaptive per-matrix rank reallocation (AR-FCCA) under a fixed budget. Cost is a one-time covariance/Fisher estimation pass over task data plus whitened eigendecomposition per adapted layer, with no increase in trainable parameters or inference latency relative to LoRA. It can fail when downstream data is too scarce for stable covariance and Fisher estimation, when the task-informed subspace does not transfer across languages, and because results are demonstrated only on Whisper and Qwen3-ASR for ASR, so applicability to other modalities is unverified.
