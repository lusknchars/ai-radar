SCOPE: Selective Critical-Step On-Policy Self-Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

SCOPE replaces trajectory-level outcome RL and full-trajectory distillation with selective self-distillation at policy-identified critical error steps, using hindsight-generated step-specific skills, a bounded teacher-student coefficient, and a length-efficiency reward. It costs an RL training pipeline with supervised warm-up, trajectory self-analysis, and environment interaction, which requires on-policy RL infrastructure (e.g., GRPO-style rollouts) that is compute-heavy even for a 3B model on a single 24 GB GPU, and the largest gains are shown with a 32B backbone that exceeds the reader's hardware. It can fail because the results are confined to two benchmarks (ALFWorld, WebShop) whose near-ceiling success rates (97-99%) may not transfer to noisier real agent tasks, the critical-step selection mechanism may misidentify error steps and inject harmful distillation signals, and the method presumes the policy can generate useful hindsight skills for its own failures.

Is it my preference? Role Grounding Attack against Agent's Memory via Social Interaction

This is an attack, not a technique to adopt: it exploits role-to-principal misattribution in LLM-agent memory systems, where a nested quoted conversation causes a victim agent to record an attacker's preference as its own user's preference, enabling tool-selection manipulation and privacy leakage. For the reader, the actionable cost is auditing any deployed memory pipeline that ingests shared or multi-party content, and adding provenance binding (explicit role and source attribution) to stored memories, which adds engineering complexity and storage overhead. What can fail: memory systems that drop conversational context at write time remain vulnerable, attribution fixes may degrade recall quality or increase latency, and defenses are unvalidated since the paper demonstrates the attack across five memory systems and six models but proposes no evaluated mitigation.

RRC replaces the corpus-derived spectral weighting rules used by PCA, Whitening, and SpecTemp with a closed-form, relevance-guided calibration that adjusts per-direction log weights via a fitted power-law trend plus a residual, without retraining the encoder or adding an inference stage. Human-Residual Steering: Aligning Frozen LLM Judges with Human Judgment Distributions

HRS replaces output-level calibration and distributional fine-tuning of LLM judges with a learned low-rank correction at a single hidden layer, predicted from the judge's hidden state and logits and trained on individual annotations with proper scoring rules. Bi-Phase Training: Learning Efficiently From Scratch

BPT replaces full-parameter pre-training of a foundation model with a parameter-efficient update scheme: frozen base weights are adapted through trainable diagonal matrices and low-rank adapters composed with constrained high-rank left/right transformations, periodically merged back into the base. SpaceAct: Benchmarking 3D Spatial and Temporal Reasoning via Closed-Loop Action and Active Perception

SpaceAct replaces passive VQA and symbolic-planning evaluations with an interactive benchmark that tests closed-loop physical action and active perception on 108 brick-and-puzzle instances without privileged coordinates. It costs the effort of setting up the SAPA evaluation environment and API calls against frontier MLLMs; it provides no deployable model or efficiency method, only measurement. It can fail as an adoption target because results on a small, specialized puzzle suite may not transfer to the reader's actual production tasks, and it measures capability gaps rather than closing them.

SCOPE: Selective Critical-Step On-Policy Self-Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

efficient training · watch · 5 of 5 implementations are independent

SCOPE replaces trajectory-level outcome RL and full-trajectory distillation with selective self-distillation at policy-identified critical error steps, using hindsight-generated step-specific skills, a bounded teacher-student coefficient, and a length-efficiency reward. It costs an RL training pipeline with supervised warm-up, trajectory self-analysis, and environment interaction, which requires on-policy RL infrastructure (e.g., GRPO-style rollouts) that is compute-heavy even for a 3B model on a single 24 GB GPU, and the largest gains are shown with a 32B backbone that exceeds the reader's hardware. It can fail because the results are confined to two benchmarks (ALFWorld, WebShop) whose near-ceiling success rates (97-99%) may not transfer to noisier real agent tasks, the critical-step selection mechanism may misidentify error steps and inject harmful distillation signals, and the method presumes the policy can generate useful hindsight skills for its own failures.
