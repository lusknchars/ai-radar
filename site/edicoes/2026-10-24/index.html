FIGFLEX: A Mixed-Precision FP-INT GEMM Accelerator Co-Designed with Low-Bit LLM Quantization

FIGFLEX replaces GPU dequantize-to-FP16 tensor-core execution of weight-only quantized models with a custom bit-parallel FP-INT accelerator whose throughput scales as weight precision falls, paired with a mixed-precision quantizer that assigns per-group bit-widths. The approach requires non-commercial accelerator hardware (an ASIC/FPGA-class design), so adopting the performance gains is impossible on a 24 GB GPU or API-based setup; only the quantizer is potentially portable, at the cost of added quantization complexity. Without the custom datapath, the claimed throughput scaling does not materialize, and the accuracy results depend on the co-designed scaling scheme that has been validated only on LLaMA-2-7B WikiText-2.

multi-agent orchestrationwatch

Governed Self-Evolution for Autonomous Investing: A Multi-Agent LLM System with an Invariant Core

The method replaces a single general-purpose LLM making trading decisions with nine role-specialised agents (four analysts, a portfolio manager, a protected risk manager), a seven-layer shared memory assembled from the system's own record, and a nightly grading plus quarterly reflection loop gated by deterministic, reversible prompt edits around an untouchable core of order arithmetic and risk rules. The cost is substantial architectural complexity: nine agent roles, seven memory layers, a nightly grading pipeline, a quarterly reflector with replay verification, and roughly 1,632 LLM calls over 102 trading days (about 16 calls per day, feasible on third-party APIs but with ongoing per-call spend). Failure modes include prompt injections embedded in untrusted Web content (tested but only within one deployment), graded self-improvement loops that reinforce their own scoring biases, replay verification that cannot cover live-market distribution shift, and single-system live results that may not generalise across market regimes.

UniGuard: Multi-View Guardrail Learning for Cross-Interface Safety Generalization

UniGuard replaces interface-specific guardrail training (fixed prompt/response formats) with multi-view training over semantically aligned prompt, conversation, agent-trace, and multi-agent representations of the same safety case, using classification, contrastive, and calibration losses. The cost is a fine-tuning run on a 0.6B model (feasible on a single 24 GB GPU) plus constructing or adopting multi-view training data; inference cost remains that of a small classifier. It can fail because the 1,000-case synthetic dataset is small, gains are reported against its own pretrained baseline rather than standard production guardrails, and synthetic multi-view transformations may not capture real agent-trace distributions in the reader's deployment.

REGATTA: A Cross-Domain Measurement Framework and Fault-Injection Harness for Enterprise AI Agents

REGATTA replaces ad hoc agent test suites and unverified compliance checklists with a source-cited checklist, a 25-fault injection corpus, and a two-stage harness that scores whether suites actually detect specified faults. Cost is limited to API-based LLM generation and judging plus the effort of auditing checklist citations against binding sources, all feasible on a small team without GPUs. What can fail: the audit found most checklist items misstated rules or had defective citations, stage agreement fell below chance (.536 vs .549) on a current-model retest, and substance review was partly AI-only, so adopted suites may embed wrong reference rules.
