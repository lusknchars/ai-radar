Language Models that Play Chess and Explain Their Moves

The method replaces prompting a general-purpose LM for domain reasoning with a hybrid architecture in which a silent expert encoder (here a chess engine) is fused into an instruction-tuned LM via cross-attention, followed by an iterative loop where the model's own analyses of candidate continuations are consolidated and distilled back into its weights. The cost is substantial: access to a pretrained expert encoder, a question-answering curriculum for cross-attention training, and repeated fine-tuning rounds of a multi-billion-parameter model, which exceeds a single 24 GB GPU for full replication though the distillation loop alone could be run on smaller models or via APIs. It can fail when no silent expert encoder exists for the target domain, when the QA curriculum fails to extract the right concepts from encoder representations, or when the self-generated explanations reinforce errors across iterations rather than improving them.

Passing the Test You Trained On: Re-evaluating Prompt-Injection Detectors for LLM Agents

The method replaces selecting prompt-injection detectors by public benchmark scores with evaluation on the agent's own tool outputs, labeled by differential replay of ground-truth tool calls, reported at a low false-positive rate, plus an audit of detector training data. Where Is the Truth When AI Agents Disagree? Dependence-Aware Belief Propagation for Unsupervised Truth Discovery (Extended Version)

BP-TD replaces plain majority vote and classical Dawid-Skene crowdsourcing estimators for combining conflicting answers from multiple LLM agents by adding per-agent decorrelation weights so that agents sharing a base model count as roughly one vote. It costs only mean-field variational inference over a one-coin model, which is computationally trivial on constrained infrastructure, but it requires maintaining a multi-agent pool whose members are meaningfully diverse rather than variants of one model. It can fail when the largest correlated bloc is actually the strongest agent group, in which case decorrelation hurts accuracy, and the authors report no label-free statistic that distinguishes this case from the favorable one.

FourSight: Stable FP4 RL Post-Training via Sequence Filtering and Sparse Channel Compensation

FourSight replaces naive NVFP4 W4A4 quantization in RL post-training by combining TIM-aware sequence filtering to exclude drifted trajectories with sparse precision compensation that keeps only high-variance activation channels in BF16, plus a fused SpliceGEMM kernel. Replaces freeform chat-transcript-driven coding agents with a staged specification-plan-task workflow where a reviewer subagent checks artefacts against fixed defect lists, an executor is restricted to declared files, and a file-inventory check runs after each task block. Costs are human approval at every stage, orchestration overhead, and reliance on the same model to both enforce and obey constraints. The main failure mode is the orchestrator judging its own checks: it logged undeclared changes in 11 of 247 block checks but halted work in only four, so enforcement can silently weaken.

Provably Learning Mixtures of Linear Experts

The paper provides a convergence analysis for jointly training gates and linear experts via gradient EM, replacing heuristic MoE training practice with a characterized recipe: logarithmic overparameterization relative to the true expert count, random gate initialization, and unconstrained mixing weights that concentrate routing so the model can later be pruned. It costs nothing to read but addresses a canonical linear-expert model under Gaussian input clusters and a separation condition, not the deep nonlinear MoE architectures used in production. The global convergence guarantee fails if clusters are not separated, and the analysis is asymptotic in character, so its predictions may not transfer to small-data, non-Gaussian, or deep-expert regimes.

Parallel Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics in Python: Task- and Data-Parallel SINDy with Numba, OpenMP, Threads and Multiprocessing for the Lorenz and Rössler Systems

The method replaces PySINDy's interpreted library evaluation and least-squares solves with Numba-JIT loop kernels that accumulate normal equations in a cache-blocked pass, parallelized via prange, PyOMP, threads, or multiprocessing. It costs implementation and maintenance of custom compiled kernels plus a QR fallback path for ill-conditioned problems, and its 117x figure was measured on a 24-core dual-socket HPC node, not commodity hardware. It can fail numerically when normal equations are ill-conditioned (mitigated by the QR fallback) and the exact-coefficient guarantee is demonstrated only on the Lorenz and Rössler benchmarks, not on noisy or large-scale real data.

Shrinkage prior for implicit generative models

Replaces standard unregularized maximum-likelihood or adversarial training of implicit generative models with a scoring-rule posterior combined with shrinkage priors and block SGMCMC within Gibbs sampling to induce sparsity. Costs include substantial implementation complexity (custom MCMC within Gibbs, scoring-rule posterior construction) and higher per-iteration compute than standard SGD, without a stated reduction in inference-time memory or latency. Can fail because the abstract offers no evidence the method transfers to production-scale architectures or standard data-regime sizes, MCMC convergence is difficult to diagnose in high dimensions, and sparsity may degrade sample quality.

LoopVQ: Sub 1-bit KV Cache Quantization for Looped Transformers

LoopVQ replaces per-step full-precision KV cache storage in looped transformers with joint vector quantization of channels across loop steps, using codebooks fine-tuned to match full-precision outputs. It costs a codebook fine-tuning stage, integration with a specific serving stack (SGLang implementation provided), and some residual accuracy loss that grows as bitrate drops toward 0.25 bits, where it must be combined with TaSQ to stay near BF16. It can fail if the deployed model is not a looped transformer (the method exploits redundancy along the loop axis, which standard LLMs lack), if loop-step sensitivity estimates do not transfer across tasks, or if codebook fine-tuning data does not cover the target distribution.

PyRUA-DataFarm: LLM Agent as a Cost-Efficient Teleoperator for Real-Ready Robot Data

The method replaces human teleoperation for collecting robot demonstration data with a coding agent that rebuilds the task in simulation, generates expert actions, and renders photorealistic videos via a video model, charging roughly $21 per task setup plus $0.80 per episode. Costs are API usage, a $5 render contract, and dependence on an external simulator, video model, and judge ensemble rather than on local GPU or cluster resources. It can fail if the simulator, video model, or judges are unavailable or too expensive, if the reconstructed scene or controller does not match real dynamics, or if judge certification passes low-quality episodes that degrade the trained policy.

Language Models that Play Chess and Explain Their Moves

distillation · test · 6 of 7 implementations are independent

The method replaces prompting a general-purpose LM for domain reasoning with a hybrid architecture in which a silent expert encoder (here a chess engine) is fused into an instruction-tuned LM via cross-attention, followed by an iterative loop where the model's own analyses of candidate continuations are consolidated and distilled back into its weights. The cost is substantial: access to a pretrained expert encoder, a question-answering curriculum for cross-attention training, and repeated fine-tuning rounds of a multi-billion-parameter model, which exceeds a single 24 GB GPU for full replication though the distillation loop alone could be run on smaller models or via APIs. It can fail when no silent expert encoder exists for the target domain, when the QA curriculum fails to extract the right concepts from encoder representations, or when the self-generated explanations reinforce errors across iterations rather than improving them.
