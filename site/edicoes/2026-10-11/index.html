Quantifying Overclaiming Propensity in Frontier LLM Agents

This is an evaluation finding, not a deployable method; it replaces the default production practice of trusting a coding agent's final response as an accurate account of its work with independent verification against tool-call logs and transcripts, using file-coverage checks and planted-defect tests as exemplars. REMORY: Learning Residual Memory for Context Compaction

REMORY replaces the plain-text summary used in context compaction with a summary plus a bounded sequence of learned soft memory tokens, appended as a residual along the sequence dimension to recover information the summary dropped. The cost is training a separate memory network per backbone LLM against a frozen model's continuation objective, plus the inference-time overhead of generating the tokens, which demands gradient-level access to the target model during training and is impractical for API-only or 27B-class models on a single 24 GB GPU. It can fail when deployed on a backbone or domain different from the training setup, since the soft tokens are model-specific and their benefit is validated mainly on summarization-attribution and two agent benchmarks rather than general workloads.

The method replaces full autoregressive plan regeneration, in which a failed step forces the entire plan to be rewritten, with a diffusion language model that regenerates only the masked, invalidated region while holding the surrounding plan steps fixed. Task-Sufficient Contraction: Source Selection for Machine Information Interfaces

The method replaces ad hoc task-agnostic compression or task-specific retraining of representations with a canonical reduced source, fixed before the encoder or rate is chosen, that merges states only when every action has identical regret, provably preserving the full rate-regret curve (and, for quadratic loss, reducing to projection onto directions in which feasible actions differ). It costs an information-theoretic analysis of the action set, loss, and feasible-action geometry per consumer task, plus a separate contraction per downstream task, and the paper offers theorems rather than implementations or benchmarks. It fails when the task's loss or action set is mis-specified, when merging is done too coarsely (the paper shows retaining only the optimal water-filled action loses information), or when approximate contractions are treated as exact.

CARE: Certifying Acceleration for Vision-Language-Action Inference

CARE replaces ad hoc selection of VLA acceleration methods (action chunking, token pruning, flow-step reduction) based on latency and average success, instead running paired rollouts from identical initial conditions on a calibration set to certify that acceleration-induced failure risk stays below a user-specified budget, deploying the fastest certified candidate or falling back to the reference policy. The cost is calibration compute: paired closed-loop rollouts must be executed for each candidate, mitigated but not eliminated by sequential testing, and the method adds selection and fallback infrastructure around the serving stack. Certification guarantees are statistical and tied to the calibration distribution, so distribution shift between calibration and deployment initial conditions can invalidate the failure bound, and a tight risk budget may leave no certified accelerator, yielding no speedup at all.

Tucker Bottleneck Attention for Multi-Dimensional Sequence Modeling

TuBA replaces full self-attention over long multidimensional token sequences (video frames, weather grids) with attention performed on compact low-rank Tucker cores, projecting hidden tensors down, mixing globally in the compressed space, and writing updates back. OnlineQAT: On-Policy Distillation for Ultra-Low-Bit Large Language Models

OnlineQAT replaces offline quantization-aware training on fixed or teacher-generated completions with a two-stage pipeline: block-wise QAT initialization followed by on-policy distillation, where a frozen full-precision teacher supplies a sampled reverse-KL signal at prefixes the quantized student itself generates. The cost is a full training recovery stage rather than post-training quantization: the teacher must be served during training, the student must generate responses on-policy (adding sampling latency per step), and one 24 GB GPU constrains this to small models such as 1.7B-scale with offloading or gradient checkpointing. It can fail if the workload cannot absorb training cost (a static PTQ method may be preferable), the marginal W2A16 gain of 0.44 points may not transfer to other models or tasks, and student sampling introduces variance and hyperparameter sensitivity absent from fixed-completion training.

It replaces storing complete per-experience records in agent memory with a task-aware allocation rule that retains only information whose downstream value exceeds what a shared world model can predict and reconstruct. Do LLMs Learn from Rewards in Context? : Rethinking the role of reward in In-Context Reinforcement Learning

The paper evaluates, rather than proposes, a technique: it tests whether appending raw trajectory-reward pairs to an LLM's context functions as inference-time RL, and finds it does not; reward signals are read but flipping, randomizing, or removing them leaves improvement curves nearly unchanged, and even corrupted trajectories help as much as real ones. It replaces nothing directly, but its finding implies that engineering effort spent on reward shaping, reward-aware prompting, and exploration instructions for context-based agent memory is largely wasted, and should instead go toward input distribution and demonstration quality as in standard ICL.
