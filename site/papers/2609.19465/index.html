Compositional Reasoning in Language Models under Reinforcement Learning Post-Training

The paper's finding replaces the intuitive curriculum of RL post-training on decomposed sub-skills first: training directly on composed tasks transfers back to decomposed skills, while the reverse transfer is unreliable. The cost is that composed-task training requires verified, compositional reward signals (here, deterministic data-structure tasks and, preliminarily, tool-calling), plus the engineering effort of building such datasets and running RL post-training on a 24 GB GPU, which limits model scale. It can fail when composed tasks are too hard to bootstrap learning from scratch, when reward verification for composed outputs is unavailable, or when the asymmetry does not hold outside the evaluated synthetic and pilot tool-calling settings.
