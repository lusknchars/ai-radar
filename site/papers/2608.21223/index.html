Event-triggered Implicit Perturbation for Zeroth-Order Fine-Tuning of Spiking Transformers

Replaces explicit read-modify-write weight perturbation with perturbation sums from dedicated hardware attached to an IMC array for zeroth-order fine-tuning of spiking networks. Compared with the simplified variant, it requires 40%-46% more silicon area and 15%-49% more energy per operation, plus custom 16 nm CMOS fabrication or synthesis. It is a circuit for SNNs, rather than software deployable on a 24 GB GPU or through a third-party API.

quality

From the paper

On CIFAR-10, PGU-XOR reaches 76.41% accuracy, versus 66.85% for PGU-Reuse and 76.53% for the software Randint reference.

Paperraft interpretation

The gain is strongest against the reuse baseline. The software reference remains slightly ahead.

Limits and unknowns

These are the authors’ spiking-model results. They do not establish quality gains for GLM or Kimi.

Next check

Repeat the same task and perturbation baselines with fixed seeds before testing another architecture.

compute and memory

From the paper

The hardware study uses a 128 × 16 in-memory array and a custom perturbation generator in 16-nm CMOS, evaluated after chip layout.

Paperraft interpretation

The reported savings concern perturbation energy. They cannot price a complete training run or an A100/H200 rental.

Limits and unknowns

GPU memory needs, total system energy, and rental cost remain unknown.

Next check

First establish a runnable implementation and measure its full memory footprint and wall-clock cost.

latency

From the paper

At 1 GHz, perturbation throughput falls from 4.10 to 0.51 TOPS as accumulation grows from one to eight cycles.

Paperraft interpretation

Smaller circuitry trades throughput for area. The paper’s no-penalty condition requires the generator to keep pace with the in-memory array.

Limits and unknowns

This is circuit throughput. Startup time, time to first token, and slow-request latency are not established.

Next check

Measure startup, median and p95 request latency separately on a complete implementation.
