## Reported claims

- Agentic multiverse analysis replaces manual specification-curve robustness checks of empirical analyses with autonomous coding agents that enumerate analytical forks, validate them against a rubric, execute the specification grid, and cross-examine each other's outputs. It costs tens of dollars and hours of agent time per paper via third-party APIs, but adds orchestration complexity including rubric design, separation-of-duties enforcement, and fork-space validation. It can fail when agents converge through imitation of exemplar reports rather than independent analysis, when the enumerated fork space is narrower than what human analysts would consider, or when the validity rubric admits weak specifications that inflate apparent robustness. (inferred)
- Full multiverse analysis of a published empirical paper completes in hours of agent time and tens of dollars per paper, a task previously requiring resources few research teams could afford; 1,755 specification estimates across ten papers reproduced all original estimates. (inferred)
