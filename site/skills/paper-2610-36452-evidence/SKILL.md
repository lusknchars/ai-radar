## Reported claims

- The method replaces velocity-commanded RL interfaces and reference-blind tracking policies with discrete stochastic motion tokens generated by a high-level controller that condition a whole-body tracking policy. Adoption requires training a motion tokenizer and RL tracking policies plus access to a physical humanoid (Unitree G1) and motion data; sim-to-real transfer and tokenizer reconstruction quality are the main costs in engineering effort. It can fail when generated token sequences request motions outside the tracking policy's training distribution, during behavior transitions the tokenizer represents poorly, or when reconstruction errors produce dynamically infeasible references on hardware. (inferred)
- At matched capacity, the stochastic motion tokenizer reconstructs Unitree G1 motions better than FSQ and VQ-VAE baselines (no numeric factor given); policies execute 16.8 s dance/fight/walk motions and a 35 s fight-to-dance sequence on a physical G1. (inferred)
