#### 1. Encoder adapter pre-training plus LoRA joint fine-tuning for ASR

It replaces direct joint fine-tuning of a Speech-LLM (or LoRA-only LLM adaptation) with a two-stage procedure: first train lightweight adapters inserted into each frozen speech-encoder layer, then jointly fine-tune the whole model with LoRA on the LLM. The cost is an extra training stage, modest additional adapter parameters in the encoder, and a more complex pipeline than a single LoRA pass; it presumes an existing Speech-LLM checkpoint and target-domain labeled audio. It can fail if the target domain is not acoustic-shifted (e.g., the gap is lexical or semantic rather than child/dialectal acoustics), if very limited target data overfits the encoder adapters, or if the base model is not an encoder-projector-LLM ASR stack, making the method inapplicable.

#### 2. KV-cache compression via test-time training

Replaces one-shot KV-cache eviction/compression heuristics with a per-document learned compact cache, optimized via test-time training using generic prompts while model weights stay frozen. Costs a per-document training pass at inference time (extra latency and compute before serving, plus engineering complexity for the two-stage distillation procedure), though memory during generation drops by the compression factor. Can fail when the per-document training cost exceeds the cache savings (short documents, single-query workloads), when generic prompts fail to cover the information later questions require, or when serving infrastructure cannot amortize the per-document training across many queries.

#### 3. Training-free composition of heterogeneous rerankers with cacheable coarse scorers

It replaces using a single expensive reranker (e.g., CrossEncoder, Provence, or ColBERTv2) alone by inserting a lightweight cacheable coarse scorer (Cache-Pointwise or Cache-Shapley) trained on passage relevance labels before it, optionally combined via reciprocal rank fusion. The cost is training the small scorer on relevance labels, maintaining cached representations, and added pipeline complexity, while online inference remains linear-time and pruning gets faster. Gains are not guaranteed: the paper reports that a cascade can underperform its coarse stage alone, and improvements depend on the reader model, reranker pairing, candidate budgets, and stage order, so a mismatched configuration can degrade accuracy.

#### 1. Program-synthesis agents for visual reasoning

Replaces prompt-conditioned video generation models, where reasoning unfolds in denoised pixels, with a coding agent that receives the same first frame and prompt and writes a program that renders the answer video for an unmodified rule-based evaluator. #### 2. Steering-and-editing augmented external memory (TASK)

TASK memory replaces text-only retrieval (BM25/vector RAG) with a triple store: a text note holding content, a removable knowledge edit serving as an in-model address keyed by subject, and a pair of content-free steering vectors that decide per query whether the model adopts the memory or its parametric answer. It costs the engineering of a knowledge-editing pipeline (e.g., rank-one edits) plus activation-steering infrastructure, requires access to model internals (ruling out API-only models), and adds steering and editing overhead per memory write and per query. It can fail through editing side effects (the paper reports up to 49% memory-misapplication when retrieval is combined with weight editing), degraded behavior on models or domains outside the four tested families, and residual version confusion when superseded facts remain stored.

After adoption, misattribution can silently misdirect review priority and risk rankings, and the claimed bug-fix prediction advantage may degrade since part of it stems from recency rather than the risk signal itself. (agentes_de_codigo · testar) arxiv.org/abs/2611.34377
- **Benchmark for embodied social intelligence in VLMs** — LUDIUS replaces ad hoc or absent evaluation of vision-language-model social judgment in embodied agents with a structured benchmark of 15 social intelligence skills tested across simulated week-long household episodes, including a harness that feeds camera views, retrieves interaction history, and converts tool calls into robot actions. It costs nothing directly to the reader but adopting its methodology requires simulation environments, episode generation, and finetuning data that are not available under a constrained infrastructure budget. Its findings indicate that frontier models fail when reasoning about what a resident perceived earlier, reconciling competing interests, or acting under safety constraints and household norms, so any agent evaluated only on simpler tasks may overestimate its readiness for socially constrained deployment. (avaliacao_de_agente · observar) arxiv.org/abs/2611.34328
- **Cross-lingual evaluation of self-evolving agents** — The paper does not replace an existing method; it introduces IRTIQA, an aligned English/Urdu/code-switched evaluation benchmark, and uses it to audit self-evolving agent frameworks (Tool-R0, AgentEvolver, Autogenesis) against their unevolved backbones. Adopting its implication means treating language parity as an explicit evaluation target, which costs building or reusing aligned multilingual test sets and, if self-evolution is pursued, RL-style training that exceeds a single 24 GB GPU budget for larger backbones. What can fail is assuming self-improvement generalizes across languages: RL-based evolution can widen the gap, hurting Urdu reasoning more than English, and code-switched inputs behave as a distinct failure condition rather than degraded translation. (avaliacao_de_agente · observar) arxiv.org/abs/2611.34303
