#### 1. Truncated automatic sparse differentiation for MLIP Hessians

The method replaces dense automatic differentiation of MLIP energies, which makes full Hessians computationally inaccessible for large systems, with sparse differentiation that exploits the finite receptive field of message-passing potentials, optionally truncating small long-range Hessian entries. Exact ASD requires computing and exploiting the sparsity pattern and delivers only modest speedups, while the practical gains come from truncated ASD, which introduces approximation by discarding nonzero entries between distant atoms and adds implementation complexity. The approach can fail when truncated couplings are not negligible for a target observable, when the MLIP lacks strict locality so the assumed sparsity does not hold, or when graph construction overhead erodes the speedup at the system sizes of interest.

#### 2. Disjoint row-sparse plus low-rank matrix decomposition

D-SLR replaces truncated SVD as the default matrix compression method for reconstruction tasks, decomposing a matrix into a low-rank component plus disjoint verbatim-stored rows selected after the fact against an error or parameter budget. It costs three SVDs total, requires no iterative solver or regularization tuning, and includes a computable a-posteriori lower bound certifying the potential gain of alternative configurations. It can fail when the data lacks a disjoint row-sparse-plus-low-rank structure, when the SVD cost is prohibitive for very large matrices, or when downstream consumers of the compressed matrix cannot accommodate the two-component format.

#### 3. RAG over incident history for LLM log root cause analysis

Replaces zero-shot and few-shot prompting of cloud or local LLMs for log-based root cause analysis with retrieval over a labeled incident history served locally via vLLM.
