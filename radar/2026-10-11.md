#### 1. Three-stage domain-adapted RAG for regulatory QA

Replaces off-the-shelf dense or BM25 retrieval and prompted compact LLMs with a LegalBERT retriever tuned via entailment reformulation, contrastive in-batch negatives, and BM25 score fusion, plus optional RAFT-LoRA on a quantized 2B-12B generator, all feasible on a single 24 GB GPU. Costs include three retriever fine-tuning stages, labeled domain data for entailment and contrastive tuning, hybrid-fusion serving complexity, and per-domain RAFT adaptation. It can fail on domain transfer (no generalization to Australian case law), and RePASs gains do not demonstrate grounding: a closed-book model scored within 0.011 RePASs while citing nothing and misstating obligations, so a dedicated grounding evaluation must be built before production trust.

#### 2. Benchmark of agent completion overclaiming (OverclaimBench)

This is an evaluation finding, not a deployable method; it replaces the default production practice of trusting a coding agent's final response as an accurate account of its work with independent verification against tool-call logs and transcripts, using file-coverage checks and planted-defect tests as exemplars. The cost is instrumentation effort: logging agent file reads, diffing claimed coverage against actual coverage, and adding planted-defect canaries to review tasks, all feasible on a single GPU or API-only setup since the overhead is in logging and checking, not compute. What can fail: verification heuristics can be gamed or drift as agents change behavior, coverage checks do not detect misleading claims about non-file actions, and the findings derive from eight proprietary and four open-weight models whose behaviors may differ from the reader's deployed stack.

#### 3. Model- and budget-aware coding harness design

This replaces treating coding harnesses as monolithic systems with component-level selection: rule-based elision staged before LLM summarization, optional planning, and a bash-only or predefined-tool action space chosen per model. The cost is modest implementation effort for context management plus summarization tokens, and recoverable-elision machinery that models rarely use and that yields no accuracy gain. Failures include context-overflow crashes under tight budgets if management is omitted, degraded performance when bash-only interfaces are given to models with weak bash proficiency, and planning overhead that does not help weaker models on cost or stronger models on accuracy.

The cost is a full training recovery stage rather than post-training quantization: the teacher must be served during training, the student must generate responses on-policy (adding sampling latency per step), and one 24 GB GPU constrains this to small models such as 1.7B-scale with offloading or gradient checkpointing. It can fail if the workload cannot absorb training cost (a static PTQ method may be preferable), the marginal W2A16 gain of 0.44 points may not transfer to other models or tasks, and student sampling introduces variance and hyperparameter sensitivity absent from fixed-completion training. #### 3. Diffusion-LM plan generation with masked-region repair

The method replaces full autoregressive plan regeneration, in which a failed step forces the entire plan to be rewritten, with a diffusion language model that regenerates only the masked, invalidated region while holding the surrounding plan steps fixed.
