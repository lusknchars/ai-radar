#### 2. Per-group WER evaluation for pruned speech models

The paper is an evaluation study, not a new compression method: it replaces the practice of selecting pruned speech-LLM checkpoints using aggregate WER alone with selection that also tracks per-demographic-group WER and the worst-performing group's error rate. The cost is additional evaluation compute (one inference pass per demographic slice per checkpoint) and extra analysis and dataset labeling, which is small relative to deployment risk. What can fail: the fairness effects are dataset-dependent (gaps widened on Fair-Speech but not clearly on Common Voice), so per-group metrics must be measured on the target population, and LoRA recovery after pruning can widen disparities even while improving every group's absolute WER.

#### 3. Clinician-defined formally verified rule-based guardrail layer

Replaces prompt engineering, LLM-as-a-Judge, and Constitutional AI safety evaluation with a clinician-authored, formally verified four-rule base applied as a guardrail whose every verdict is recomputable from sixteen named facts. Costs: clinician time to define and adjudicate rules, a fact-extraction step, and a measured over-refusal regression where a specification revision raised block-class outcomes on held-out boundary prompts from 6.0% to 16.0%, plus an uncertainty default that can over-block. Can fail when fact extraction misidentifies content (the main source of the over-refusal regression) and when the fixed rule set misses safety failures outside its four checks, where the LLM judge may remain stronger.

It can fail as an adoption decision if the reader's agents do not modify existing frontend codebases, since the continuity signal is specific to Change tasks on live repositories, and benchmark scores on its 400 tasks may not transfer to a proprietary codebase without constructing equivalent staged checks. (avaliacao_de_agente · testar) arxiv.org/abs/2610.31118
- **Dual-track benchmark for agents and world models** — Dual Exam replaces ad hoc or single-component agent evaluation with a controlled benchmark that scores action selection and state evolution separately across 1,088 agent items and 400 world-model cases. It costs only evaluation compute and API calls to the listed configurations, which fits a single-GPU or API-budget team, but requires implementing the harness and interpreting non-comparable indices. It can fail to predict real coupled deployments: the 150-case pilot recorded zero task successes, and the authors state its value for predicting coupled success or sustained reciprocal learning remains unestablished. (avaliacao_de_agente · testar) arxiv.org/abs/2610.31066
- **Compiling policies into neurosymbolic predicate programs** — SCOPE replaces fine-tuned guardrail models and LLM-as-a-judge policy evaluation by compiling each policy into an executable program of small neural predicates, deterministic extractors, and explicit compositional logic, optimized against adversarially generated counterexamples. It costs the upfront effort of policy compilation and counterexample-driven optimization, plus maintaining the execution pipeline; claimed benefits are higher accuracy than prompt optimization or workflow generation baselines, transfer to inexpensive small models without re-optimization, and more stable, interpretable verdicts than frontier reasoning models. It can fail on policies that do not decompose cleanly into narrow predicates, when adversarial training data undercovers the policy space, and if the predicate extractors drift against real production traffic. (seguranca_e_guardrails · testar) arxiv.org/abs/2610.31062
- **Clause-aware semantic retrieval for tool routing** — The method replaces direct large-model tool selection with clause-aware aggregated semantic retrieval over ChromaDB to choose the smallest complete subset of executable robot capabilities. It costs roughly 10 points of composite routing quality (0.7822 versus 0.8869) and requires maintaining an embedded tool registry and annotated routing data, in exchange for lower latency, local deployment, and no per-call API cost. It can fail on compound instructions through incomplete selection that omits necessary sensing or monitoring tools, or through over-selection that exposes irrelevant capabilities, especially as the tool registry grows beyond the 164-prompt evaluation regime. (uso_de_ferramenta · testar) arxiv.org/abs/2610.31013
- **LLM-bounded simulation optimization for advisories** — It replaces expert-driven, static packages of practice with rolling in-season advisories in which LLM agents bound actionable agronomic parameters and an optimizer searches them against a crop simulator (APSIM) acting as an evaluation oracle. The cost is substantial integration complexity: licenses and calibration for a crop simulator, per-farm soil and weather data pipelines, multi-agent LLM API calls at every advisory interval, and optimizer compute per rollout. It can fail where simulator calibration drifts from local field conditions, where LLM-proposed bounds are infeasible or unsound, or where weather analogs are unrepresentative, and the reported gains are validated only for maize at two sites. (orquestracao_multiagente · observar) arxiv.org/abs/2610.31003
