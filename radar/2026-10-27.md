#### 1. Linear-memory pruned Viterbi forced alignment

The method replaces quadratic-time-and-space Viterbi forced alignment (as in torchaudio) with a Hirschberg-based in-place variant plus constrained-random-walk search-space pruning, enabling alignment of multi-hour audio on CPU-class hardware. The cost is implementation complexity beyond a standard Viterbi decoder and reliance on an existing acoustic model to supply frame-level emissions; the alignment quality itself is unchanged without pruning. With pruning enabled, alignment accuracy can degrade in roughly 2% of tested cases, particularly where transcriptions contain errors that the random-walk model does not capture.

#### 2. Interaction-aware prefix correction for quantized agentic RL

InterQ-RL replaces current-token rollout-learner ratio correction (as in prior quantized-RL methods) with a bounded correction applied over the full state-reaching action history, distributing correction strength across segments under a fixed displacement budget. It requires no additional model forward pass, so the cost is implementation complexity in the importance-ratio computation rather than added memory or latency; W8A8-KV8 rollout itself reduces generation and KV-cache cost during long multi-turn rollouts. It can fail if the reader is not doing multi-turn agent RL at all (the correction targets execution mismatch along interaction histories, irrelevant to single-turn or SFT pipelines), if bounded prefix correction mis-allocates strength on environments with very long or sparse action segments, or if the reader's small models and environments differ enough from ALFWorld/WebShop/ScienceWorld with 4B-30B backbones that the mismatch it fixes is negligible.

#### 3. Mean-span readout with selective adapter pruning for NLI reranking

The method replaces option-ending pointer readouts with mean pooling over the contextualized states inside each NLI option, and replaces full all-module adapter tuning with low-rank updates restricted to attention and gated DeltaNet projections, dropping feed-forward adapters. It adds no head parameters and reduces trainable parameters by more than half, lowering fine-tuning memory and compute while claiming higher reranking accuracy. It can fail because results are reported on a single 4B model and one aggregate benchmark (MC11), so gains may not transfer to other backbones, domains, or reranking workloads.
